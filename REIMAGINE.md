# REIMAGINE

**Names:** Owen, Kaif

## Problem

"Uncritical Acceptance Risk": students need deeper understanding of, and autonomy over, their own decisions and Claude's suggestions.

## Evidence

41.7% of students (5 of 12) admitted to agreeing to Claude's suggestions without fully understanding what they were consenting to. 25.0% (3) were neutral and 33.3% (4) disagreed.

## Solutions, pros and cons

| Solution | Pro | Con |
|---|---|---|
| **Mutual criticism:** Claude critiques parts of your suggestions, and if Claude makes a suggestion you have to critique it. | You're forced to understand what makes solutions correct or incorrect, and to think deeper about them. | Annoying. |
| **Do the first part of the assignment/activity by hand, without Claude.** | You literally have to understand it and produce the responses on your own. | The activities are built around working with Claude, so designing one part to be independent of the rest might be challenging. |
| **Switch who's in the driver's seat.** Instead of Claude asking the questions, you give an intended end result and instructions to build that result with Claude. | When Claude asks every question, you can treat the assignment like a problem set, with no understanding of the whole project you create. | Grading becomes more complicated, and probably needs a completely new rubric. |

## What we built

We built solution 3. Claude wrote [part2instructions.md](part2instructions.md), which replaces the original Parts 2 to 5. It states a final goal and breaks the work into milestones (a brief, evidence, an analysis tool, investigation, a challenge to Claude's claims, and a report). Each milestone has you draft your own conclusions and direct Claude through the assignment, rather than the other way around. It includes a sample rubric, which addresses solution 3's grading con. [suggested_claude_md.md](suggested_claude_md.md) holds the `CLAUDE.md` rules that make Claude build from your brief and log which decisions were yours.

## What happened when we tried it

Claude produced a large instruction sheet with milestones that have you draft your own conclusions and direct Claude through the assignment. For an activity like this, it might significantly extend the expected time. Applied to the crucial parts of homeworks or activities like this, though, it should extend how much the student is forced to engage critically with the assignment.
